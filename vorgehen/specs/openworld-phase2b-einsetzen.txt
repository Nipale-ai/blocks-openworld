Wire the finished 3D assets into the game.

The game is in this directory (`src/`, builds with `node build.mjs`). The assets
are in `phase2-assets/out/` — 22 GLB files with PBR textures, built against the
contract in `NOTES.md` section 5. `phase2-assets/NOTES-assets.md` documents each
file and how it is meant to be loaded.

This is the payoff round: everything so far has been placeholder boxes. When you
are done the game must look like the renders.

## What to do

1. **Replace the placeholder builders in `src/assets.js`** with GLTF loads.
   The contract was written for exactly this: same names, same pivots, same
   orientations, same child objects. Gameplay code must not need a single
   change. Where an asset does not match the contract, fix the loading code —
   do not change gameplay to accommodate it.
2. **Load asynchronously and start fast.** Do not block the game on a 20 MB
   download. Show something immediately, swap in assets as they arrive, or hold
   a short loading screen with real progress — your call, but a player must not
   stare at a blank page.
3. **Instance what repeats.** Buildings, props, civilians, traffic — hundreds of
   copies must not become hundreds of draw calls. The LOD variants
   (`*_lod1.glb`) exist for distance; use them.
4. **Keep the dusk lighting working.** The materials arriving now are real PBR
   sets, so they will react differently to the lights than the flat vertex
   colours did. Retune where needed: the city must still read at dusk, street
   lights must still pool, wet asphalt must still reflect. Do not lose what the
   last round achieved.
5. **Ship it as a folder, not a single file.** Assets load from disk next to
   the HTML. This replaces the one-file constraint — it exists so textures can
   stream and the page can start instantly.
6. **Compress everything on the way out.** Use `gltf-transform`
   (`npx @gltf-transform/cli@latest`, verified working, version 4.5.0):
   Draco or Meshopt for geometry, **KTX2/Basis for every texture**. This is not
   optional — uncompressed PNG textures decompress fully in GPU memory and will
   sink the frame rate on ordinary machines. Report the before/after sizes.

## VERIFY AS YOU GO — not just at the end

This is the explicit instruction from the person who commissioned this: check
and test **continuously**, not only when you think you are finished. Twenty-two
assets go in during this round. If you wire them all in and test at the end,
you will be debugging twenty-two things at once and you will miss some.

**After each group of assets — vehicles, then weapons, then characters, then
buildings and props — stop and verify that group before moving on:**

- Build, load the game headless in real Chrome, and take screenshots that
  actually show what you just wired in.
- **Open those screenshots and look at them.** Wrong scale, wrong orientation,
  black materials, missing textures, a mesh at the origin instead of its
  position, a car sunk into the road — all obvious in an image, all invisible
  in a log.
- Check the frame rate and the console after each group, not only at the end.
  Then you know which group cost what.
- Fix what is wrong before you add the next group.

**At the end, run the full pass again**: walk, drive all four vehicles, open the
weapon wheel, fire all three weapons, trigger a police chase to five stars, get
out on foot among civilians. Screenshot each of those states, look at every
image, and compare against the old shots in `shots/`.

**Do not report anything as done that you have not looked at.** A green check
that no one looked at is exactly how the previous round shipped a police car
with mirrored lettering.

## Known defect to fix

`vehicle_police.glb`: the "POLICE" lettering on the doors is **mirrored** — it
reads backwards on both sides. Found by looking at
`phase2-assets/out/renders/vehicle_police_sheet.png`; the asset's own report is
green because it only checks numbers. Fix the UVs or the decal placement, and
look at the result.

## Rules that still bind

- **Bright enough to see.** Same measurement as every round: screenshot the
  running game, under a third of the frame near-black, road and geometry clearly
  visible.
- **60 fps.** Real models cost more than boxes. If something has to give, drop
  detail in the distance, not the frame rate. Report what you measure.
- Zero console errors.
- Play it headless with real Chrome and **look at the screenshots**: walk, drive
  each vehicle, open the weapon wheel, fire all three weapons, trigger a police
  chase. Compare against the old shots in `shots/` — this must be visibly
  better, not just different.
- Update NOTES.md: how assets load, what you retuned, compressed sizes, frame
  rate, and what still looks weak.

The character model is the weakest asset (tube arms with a visible shoulder
seam, stub hands). Note it in NOTES.md; do not rebuild it in this round.
