Continue the open-world game in this directory. Source in `src/`, builds with
`node build.mjs`, NOTES.md documents every system. This round is **purely about
how the game looks**. Do not change the gameplay, the systems, or the feature
set. Nothing new gets added — the existing world gets a new look.

**DO NOT SSH ANYWHERE. DO NOT USE ANY GPU.** systema, brain, spark and ComfyUI
are off limits — that machine belongs to someone else right now. Everything in
this round is runtime rendering, written by hand.

## The problem, stated honestly

The game currently runs under flat daylight. Under an even overhead sun, simple
geometry looks its worst: nothing is picked out, nothing is in shadow, every
surface reads as the same matte plane. Compare it to interior scenes lit by
practical lights and ours looks unfinished — not because the geometry is worse,
but because the lighting does nothing for it.

## Move the whole city to dusk

Not full night — **the blue hour**. Sun just below the horizon, sky still
carrying colour, everything else dark enough that artificial light reads.
The city must still be legible: you have to see where you are driving.

This is the change everything else depends on, so get the sky and the ambient
level right first, then build the rest of the lighting on top.

## What must appear once it is dark enough to matter

- **Street lights that actually light the street** — visible pools of light on
  the road with dark gaps between them, warm sodium against the cold sky.
  Instanced, so hundreds of them stay affordable.
- **Car headlights and tail lights.** Beams that fall on the road and on other
  cars, brake lights that brighten. Traffic at dusk should read as strings of
  moving light. Police lights must sweep the buildings around them.
- **Windows lit in buildings** — not all of them, and not all the same colour.
  A tower with a scattering of warm windows reads as inhabited.
- **Neon and signage** — emissive faces on shops and hoardings, colours that
  spill onto what is near them.
- **Wet asphalt.** Roads should be visibly more reflective than pavements, so
  the lights above them smear into the surface. This is a material property
  (low roughness, correct metalness) plus screen-space reflections or a cheaper
  approximation — pick what holds 60 fps.
- **Shadows that anchor things.** Objects need contact shadows; without them
  everything looks pasted onto the ground. Ambient occlusion at contacts,
  cascaded shadow maps tuned so the near range is crisp.

## Post-processing

The composite pass exists in `postfx.js`. Bring it up:
bloom tuned to the emissive sources rather than blanket glow, colour grading
that separates cold sky from warm practicals, subtle vignette, and enough
contrast that the frame has actual blacks. It should look graded, not filtered.

## Rules that still bind

- **It must remain playable and readable.** Dusk is not an excuse for a dark
  screen. Measure the brightness of the running game as before: under a third of
  the frame near-black, the road and the geometry the player moves through
  clearly visible. This is the same gate that has been running all night and it
  does not move.
- **Hold 60 fps.** Report what you measure. If a lighting feature costs more
  than it gives, drop it and say so in NOTES.md.
- Zero console errors.
- **Change nothing about gameplay.** No new mechanics, no rebalancing, no
  feature work. If a system needs a small hook to expose its lights (headlights,
  police beacons), add the hook and nothing more.
- Screenshot the running game from the same viewpoints as the existing shots in
  `shots/` so the before/after is directly comparable, and look at them. Keep
  going until it looks like something worth showing.

Update NOTES.md with what you changed, what you measured, what you dropped for
performance, and which parts would improve further once real textures and baked
lighting arrive in the GPU phase.
