## What to build

Work through these in order of visual impact. Finish each one properly before
starting the next — three excellent assets beat eight rough ones.

1. **The three player vehicles** — sedan, muscle car, van. Then the **police
   cruiser**. Real car shapes: bodywork with panel definition, wheel arches,
   glass, lights, bumpers, grille, mirrors, interior visible through the
   windows. Keep every named child the contract requires (`body`, `lights`,
   `brakeLights`, `wheel_FL/FR/RL/RR`, the `seat`/`exit_L`/`exit_R`/`exhaust`/
   `headlight_L/R` empties, and on the cruiser `beacon_L`/`beacon_R`).
2. **The three weapons** — pistol, machine gun, rocket launcher. These are seen
   closest to camera, so they carry the most detail per triangle. Grip at the
   origin, barrel down +Z, `muzzle` empty at the tip, `loadedRocket` on the
   launcher.
3. **The character rig** — player, civilians, officers. Same bone names, same
   rest orientation, same joint positions as the contract. Real proportions and
   clothing, not a box stack. Officers need a recognisable uniform, cap and belt.
4. **Buildings** — the kinds listed in the contract. These are instanced across
   the whole city, so they decide how the skyline reads. Facade relief, window
   frames, ledges, roof detail. Keep footprints and heights exactly.
5. **Street furniture and props** if time remains — lamps, signs, benches,
   barriers, bins, hydrants.

## How to build it

- **Blender headless, scripted** (`blender -b -P script.py`). No clicking.
  Keep every generator script in `phase2-assets/` so the work can be repeated.
- **Export glTF/GLB per asset**, textures packed, Y up, +Z forward, correct
  scale in metres. One file per asset, named clearly.
- **Budget triangles like a real game does**: the weapons and the player car can
  afford detail, buildings are instanced hundreds of times and must stay lean.
  Normal maps carry detail that geometry should not. State your budgets and what
  you hit in NOTES-assets.md.
- **Materials as proper PBR sets** — base colour, roughness, metalness, normal.
  Generate them in ComfyUI where that beats procedural work, and bake procedural
  Blender materials to textures where that is better. Car paint needs real
  metalness and low roughness; asphalt and concrete need the opposite.
- **UV unwrap everything** you texture, and keep a second UV channel free on
  static geometry so lightmaps can be baked into it later.

## Verify your own work — this is where these runs fail

- After every export, **load the GLB back** and render it: a turntable or three
  fixed angles, on a neutral ground, lit plainly. **Open the images and look at
  them.** Wrong scale, flipped normals, missing material, broken pivot and
  inside-out geometry are all obvious in a render and invisible in a log.
- **Check the contract numerically**: print each asset's bounding box, origin
  offset, and the world positions of every named child, and compare them against
  the table in NOTES.md. A wheel 4 cm off ruins the suspension.
- Throw away and redo anything that does not look convincing. You have the GPU
  and the time; a mediocre asset that ships is worse than one more iteration.
