Build the complete playable system for a third-person open-world action game.
One self-contained HTML file, game.html, in the current working directory.
Everything embedded — it runs offline from the file system, no server.

READ THIS FIRST — WHAT THIS PHASE IS

This is phase one of two. The render workstation is busy right now, so this
phase is about the GAME, not the art. You are building what a studio calls a
graybox: every system complete and tuned, with simple geometry standing in for
final assets.

**DO NOT SSH ANYWHERE. DO NOT USE ANY GPU. DO NOT TRY TO REACH systema, brain,
spark, or ComfyUI.** That machine is in use by someone else and touching it is
the one thing that ruins this run. Everything here is built locally with code.

In phase two, a later session will replace your placeholder meshes with modelled
assets from Blender, your flat colours with generated PBR textures, and bake
lightmaps into the static world. **So build for that handover.** Keep asset
creation in clearly named, isolated functions — `makePlayerMesh()`,
`makeVehicleMesh(type)`, `makeWeaponMesh(type)`, `makeBuilding(...)` — each
returning a THREE.Object3D with correct scale, correct origin, and correct
attachment points. Someone must be able to swap the body of those functions for
a GLTF load without touching a single line of gameplay code. Write down the
expected dimensions and pivot of every one of them in NOTES.md.

WHAT MUST WORK BY THE END OF THIS PHASE

1. Third-person character on foot: walk, run, sprint, jump. A follow camera that
   does not clip through geometry, does not jitter, and does not fight the
   player. Tune this until it feels good — it is the thing everything else sits
   on.
2. Cars you get into and out of. Walk up, press a key, the character enters, the
   camera becomes a driving camera, you drive, press again to exit. Driving must
   have weight: acceleration curve, mass, grip, handbrake, suspension that
   reacts to the road. This should feel like driving, not like sliding a box.
3. Three vehicles that handle differently — a sedan, a muscle car, a van or
   truck. Different mass, grip and acceleration, and different placeholder
   silhouettes so they are already distinguishable.
4. A weapon wheel held open on a key, slowing time, with pistol, machine gun and
   rocket launcher. Each fires differently: rate, recoil, spread, projectile,
   impact. Recoil must move the camera and the aim. The rocket launcher must
   feel heavy and slow; the pistol must not. Hit feedback on everything you can
   shoot.
5. NPCs walking the world on their own, pathing around obstacles, reacting to
   the player and to gunfire.
6. A city block layout worth moving through: streets, junctions, buildings with
   real volume, places that are interesting to drive between. Get the SHAPE of
   the city right now — in phase two it gets its skin, and a bad layout cannot
   be fixed with textures.
7. Two more mechanics of your choosing, built properly. Things that make the
   world feel alive, not things that add menus.
8. Sound: synthesise everything in Web Audio — gunfire, impacts, engine note
   following actual RPM, footsteps, tyre squeal. Layered transients, pitch
   variation per shot, distance falloff, real low-end on the rocket launcher.
   Do not generate audio files; there is no GPU in this phase. Leave a clearly
   marked hook where generated radio music will be dropped in later.

QUALITY BAR

- **It must be bright enough to see.** Screenshot the running game, measure
  actual pixel brightness, and confirm the ground and the geometry the player
  moves through are clearly visible — not just the sky. If more than a third of
  the frame is near-black, the lighting is wrong: fix it and measure again.
- Even with placeholder geometry this should look deliberate: a coherent palette,
  readable silhouettes, real-time lights and shadows tuned properly, ACES tone
  mapping, bloom that is adjusted rather than switched on. Grayboxes can look
  good. Make this one look good.
- Instancing for anything repeated. Keep the frame budget healthy from the
  start — it only gets more expensive in phase two.
- Drive it headless (Playwright with real Chrome) and actually play it: walk,
  enter a car, drive, open the wheel, fire all three weapons, get shot at.
  Screenshot each state, look at the screenshots, fix what is wrong, repeat.
- Target 60 fps and report what you measure, not what you hope. Zero console
  errors.

CONSTRAINTS

- One file, game.html, everything inlined.
- Work in files on disk and assemble at the end. Do not emit the whole game in
  one response.
- Keyboard and mouse. Show the controls in-game.
- NOTES.md: what you built, how each system works, the exact dimensions and
  pivots of every placeholder mesh, where the asset hooks are, what you measured,
  what you would do with more time. This file is the handover to phase two —
  write it for the person who has to swap your placeholders for real models.

Take the time this needs. A tight, good-feeling graybox is worth far more than a
rushed one with more features.
