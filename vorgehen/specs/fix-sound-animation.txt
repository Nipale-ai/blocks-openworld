## 1. The vehicle and siren sounds are too shrill

His words: the car sound and the police siren are too shrill. That is the most
irritating thing in the build right now — it is the first thing anyone hears.

The audio is synthesised in `src/audio.js`. Shrill almost always means the same
things, so go through them:

- **Too much energy in the upper harmonics.** Roll them off. A real engine is
  dominated by its fundamental and the first few harmonics; the high ones sit
  far below, not level with them.
- **No low end.** An engine must be felt, not just heard. Put real weight below
  120 Hz and let it rise with load, not only with RPM.
- **No low-pass anywhere.** Add filtering that closes as the sound moves away
  and opens when it is near, and make it react to load rather than staying static.
- **The siren is a special case.** A real siren is loud but not piercing: its
  tone is narrow-band and the sweep is smooth. Sawtooth or square waves with
  full harmonics through a hard-edged sweep are exactly what hurts. Soften the
  waveform, narrow the band, and make distance actually dull it.
- Check the overall level balance too: engine and siren must sit under the
  radio and the gunfire, not over them.

**Verify by measuring, not by hoping:** render each sound through an
`OfflineAudioContext`, compute the spectral centroid and the energy above
4 kHz, and compare before and after. Shrillness is measurable — show that it
went down. Also confirm the low end actually gained energy.

## 2. The character animation is too stiff

His words: the movements and animations of the character are too stiff.

The animation is procedural, on the rig bones, in `src/player.js`. Stiffness in
procedural animation comes from a small set of causes — work through them:

- **Everything moves on the same beat.** Offset the phases: hips lead, chest
  follows, arms trail, head last. Nothing in a body starts at the same instant.
- **No secondary motion.** Add follow-through and overshoot: the chest twists
  against the hips, arms swing past their target and settle back, the head
  counter-rotates slightly against the torso.
- **Damping constants all identical.** Different masses settle at different
  rates — a head is not a forearm.
- **No weight.** There should be a vertical bob tied to the stride, a slight
  drop on the down-step, lean into acceleration and against deceleration.
- **Transitions snap.** Blend between idle, walk, run and sprint over time
  rather than switching, and let the blend follow actual speed.
- **The idle is dead.** Standing still is never still: breathing, small weight
  shifts, occasional glances.

Also make turning readable — the body should rotate into a turn rather than
pivoting on the spot.

**Verify by looking:** record short headless captures of walking, running,
sprinting, turning and standing idle, extract frames, and look at them in
sequence. Stiffness is visible in a frame strip: identical poses, no
overlap between limbs, no vertical movement.
